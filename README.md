## 🎯 Project Overview

Terranova 4G 100 is a national digital-inclusion investment programme designed to bring commercial-grade 4G mobile coverage to **100 rural communities** across the Spanish provinces of Asturias, A Coruña, Lugo, and Pontevedra. The mandate: install, commission, and hand over to operations 100 new radio base stations — each built to the same standard of quality, safety, and regulatory compliance — on a compressed **14-month calendar**.

The underlying planning documentation reflects that scale: **9,057 individually sequenced tasks**, ten parallel deployment crews, and a **€4.56M** capital and operating envelope, all governed through a single PMBOK 8th Edition-aligned Project Management Plan.

## ⚡ Key Features

- 🏗️ **Standardized, Replicable Site-Delivery Model** — the same rigorous sequence executed 100 times, tuned locally but never improvised
- 🔀 **Hybrid Waterfall + Agile Governance** — waterfall discipline through Initiating and Planning, Agile/Kanban rhythm through Executing
- 👥 **Ten Parallel Cross-Functional Crews** — each Infrastructure, RF, Transmission, and Electrical crew paired from day one with its Operations & Maintenance counterpart
- 🗺️ **Decentralized Negotiation & Permitting** — dedicated Negotiation and Permit Analysts travel with every crew, pursuing landlord and municipal clearances in parallel with engineering
- 📊 **Task-Level Earned Value Management** — BCWS, BCWP, and ACWP rolled up into CPI and SPI at every level of a six-level WBS, from a single field task to the programme total
- 🧩 **9,057-Task Master Schedule** — planned and tracked in MS Project Professional across six outline levels
- 📈 **24-Report, Four-Layer Dashboard Suite** — purpose-built views for Executive/Sponsor, Management/PMO, Project Manager, and Project Team audiences
- 🌍 **Socio-Economic Impact Focus** — closing the connectivity gap behind "España vaciada" (depopulated Spain) through telemedicine, e-commerce, and tele-education access

## 🧭 Business Challenge

Rural Spain has been losing population and connectivity investment in tandem for decades — a dynamic widely known in Spanish public discourse as *"España vaciada."* Commercial operators have historically prioritized urban and peri-urban build-out, where subscriber density and return on capital are highest. That logic is rational at the level of a single site, but it leaves entire municipalities — older, dispersed, and dependent on remote services — without reliable mobile coverage, accelerating the very depopulation that caused operators to deprioritize them in the first place.

### The Problem

Rural connectivity programmes combine the technical and regulatory complexity of a national telecom build-out with the socio-economic sensitivity of working, site by site, inside small communities — where a single delayed permit or strained landlord relationship can stall an entire local deployment. Four pressures recur across the portfolio:

| Pressure | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| **Fragmented land tenure** | Negotiation happens with individual landowners, not institutional real-estate counterparties |
| **Dispersed permitting authority** | Clearances are spread across dozens of municipal and provincial bodies, not one regulator |
| **Terrain & grid-access variability** | Every site is technically unique even inside a standardized process |
| **Fixed public-investment deadline** | Universal-service commitments leave little room for schedule slippage to compound quietly across 100 parallel workstreams |

Without mobile broadband, these communities are effectively excluded from three services increasingly treated as basic infrastructure elsewhere in Europe: **telemedicine**, **e-commerce and digital financial services**, and **tele-education** — and permitting risk, not engineering risk, is often the true long pole in the schedule.

## 📦 Scope of Work

The scope spans the full delivery chain of a greenfield mobile network build-out, replicated with local variation at every one of the 100 locations:

- **Site Acquisition** — desktop pre-survey, three-option site search, landlord negotiation, cadastral and urban-planning review
- **Technical Site Survey (TSSR)** — geotechnical, RF/line-of-sight, electrical/grid-connection, and civil/structural surveys, compiled into a formal report approved before design proceeds
- **Permitting** — cultural-heritage clearance, right-of-way and sectorial clearance, grid-connection permits, environmental impact assessment, municipal and construction permits
- **Procurement** — negotiation and permitting services, infrastructure, electrical, transmission, RF, and core network equipment, run through formal technical evaluation and vendor award
- **Construction & Installation** — structure manufacturing and foundation, shelter and perimeter work, medium-voltage connection and grounding, backhaul installation, RF equipment installation and commissioning
- **Core Network** — capacity upgrade to absorb traffic from 100 new sites, plus VoLTE licensing activation
- **Handover** — acceptance testing, documentation update, and transition to the paired Operations & Maintenance crew

The schedule descends as far as **six outline levels** — from the four-phase programme structure down to individual field activities such as *"grounding system installation"* or *"notarize contract"* — planned over a **439-calendar-day window** from January 2026 through September 2027.

## 🏛️ Solution Architecture

Every site is delivered through an identical technical sequence — the reason a 100-site rollout is manageable rather than a hundred bespoke projects.

![Deployment Process](./images/02-deployment-process.png)

**The sequence, at every site:**

1. **Desktop pre-survey** — verify GPS coordinates, define search radius
2. **Site search & acquisition** — three candidate locations evaluated in parallel, preferred option taken to negotiation
3. **Four concurrent surveys** — geotechnical, RF/line-of-sight, electrical/grid-connection, civil/structural — converging into a single Technical Site Survey Report, formally approved by the Engineering Management Team
4. **Construction sequence** — Infrastructure → Electrical → Transmission → RF works, each closed out with a formal acceptance test procedure
5. **Core network upgrade** — executed centrally, in parallel with per-site construction
6. **Handover** — ten paired O&M crews receive each site directly from the crew that built it

### Solution Model

A standardized, replicable site-delivery model executed by **ten parallel, cross-functional crews**. Each crew works a rotating slate of sites through the identical survey → permitting → procurement → construction → commissioning sequence, with a dedicated Negotiation Analyst and Permit Analyst embedded in every crew — pursuing landlord agreements and municipal clearances in parallel with engineering work rather than gating it. The result: a production line for rural connectivity, the same rigorous process repeated 100 times.

## 🔄 Methodology Alignment

Terranova 4G 100 is governed through a deliberate **two-stage methodology**: a waterfall backbone for Initiating and Planning, anchored in the latest **PMBOK 8th Edition**, followed by an **Agile, Kanban-driven** operating rhythm through Executing.

| Phase | Duration | Investment | Governing Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Initiating** | 30 days | €22,968 | Charter, governance, and stakeholder foundation |
| **2 — Planning** | 102 days | €918,844 | Site survey (100×) and full PMBOK planning suite |
| **3 — Executing** | 331 days | €3,606,400 | Procurement, core upgrade, 100-site deployment |
| **4 — Closing** | 26 days | €8,960 | Acceptance, documentation, and formal closure |

### Initiating Phase (30 days)
Business case, benefits management plan, and existing agreements reviewed alongside regulatory and rural-zone context. A dedicated socio-economic feasibility study and initial CapEx/OpEx analysis ground the business case and inform the choice of the hybrid waterfall/Agile development approach. Closes with a sponsor-approved Project Charter, elaborated Stakeholder Register, and initial Assumption Log.

### Planning Phase (102 days)
The most process-intensive phase. Opens with the Technical Site Survey replicated across all 100 candidate sites, running concurrently with requirements elicitation and initial subsidiary-plan drafting. Works methodically through the full PMBOK planning sequence — scope, schedule, cost, budget, resource, communications, stakeholder, and risk management — producing the WBS that expands to 9,057 tasks and the €4.56M programme budget. Closes with an integrated Project Management Plan and formal sponsor approval.

### Executing Phase (331 days)
The largest phase by duration and budget (€3.6M). Opens with an 83-day, six-workstream procurement phase under Evaluation Committee and Trade Compliance oversight. The centerpiece is the 317-day, €3.45M **100 4G Site Deployment** workstream, in which ten paired crews execute the technical sequence at each site — tracked on Kanban boards that give the programme office a live, visual read on how many sites sit in negotiation, permitting, construction, or commissioning at any given moment.

### Monitoring & Controlling Phase (continuous)
Consistent with PMBOK 8th Edition's shift toward continuously operating performance domains, this is not a discrete phase but a discipline embedded in every one of the 9,057 tasks — each carrying BCWS, BCWP, and ACWP rolled up into CPI and SPI at every WBS level.

### Closing Phase (26 days)
Formal verification of deliverables against acceptance criteria, vendor contract closure, crew release, a structured lessons-learned exercise, and final sponsor sign-off confirming 100 commissioned sites delivered within budget and schedule baseline.

## 🤝 Stakeholder Alignment

A programme spanning dozens of municipal authorities, individual landowners, equipment vendors, and internal engineering, financial, legal, and network-operations functions cannot be aligned informally.

- **Formal Stakeholder Register** elaborated immediately after charter approval, followed by four subsidiary plan updates — Requirements, Communications, Risk, and Stakeholder Engagement Management
- **Paired accountability** — every external vendor relationship (Infrastructure, Electrical, RF, Transmission) carries a named internal QA Manager against a named vendor Project Manager
- **Decentralized landlord and municipal engagement** — every crew carries its own Negotiation Analyst and Permit Analyst, building community-facing relationships locally rather than remotely
- **Layered vendor governance** — Legal Counsel, Trade Compliance Specialist, Procurement Manager, Evaluation Committee, and Technical Evaluation Team validate every award decision against the approved Technical Site Survey Reports

This structure keeps engineering, commercial, legal, and community stakeholders synchronized without escalating every decision to central governance — reserving executive and sponsor attention for the exceptions that genuinely need it.

## 🌍 Programme Impact

**For the operator**, Terranova 4G 100 converts a historically unprofitable class of rural sites into a disciplined, cost-predictable programme — 100 sites delivered under a single governance framework at a blended cost a scattered, one-off approach could never achieve, while building a repeatable delivery model that shortens the business case for the next rural expansion phase.

**For the communities**, the impact is more immediate and human: reliable mobile coverage is the on-ramp to

- 🏥 **Telemedicine** — sparing elderly residents a two-hour drive to the nearest hospital
- 🛒 **E-commerce & digital banking** — keeping rural economies connected to national and export markets
- 🎓 **Tele-education** — keeping schooling options open for families who would otherwise face the choice between rural life and their children's education

Closing that gap is, in the end, the programme's real deliverable.

## 💡 Key Takeaways

- **Standardization is what makes scale possible.** A single, replicable site-delivery sequence — run identically 100 times — is what turns a 100-site rollout into a manageable programme instead of a hundred bespoke projects.
- **Permitting risk, not engineering risk, is the true critical path** in a rural, multi-jurisdiction build-out. Embedding a Permit Analyst and Negotiation Analyst inside every crew — rather than centralizing that function — keeps land and municipal clearances moving in parallel with engineering instead of gating it.
- **Waterfall and Agile are complementary, not competing, choices.** A rigorous waterfall foundation retires planning risk once; an Agile, Kanban-driven Executing phase then absorbs the inevitable site-by-site variability of rural construction without losing overall schedule control.
- **Reporting has to be layered by audience, not centralized in one dashboard.** A Sponsor deciding whether to release the next funding tranche and a crew lead deciding which task to pick up next need fundamentally different views of the same underlying schedule.
- **Task-level Earned Value turns a schedule into a control instrument.** Rolling BCWS/BCWP/ACWP up through six WBS levels lets the programme office say precisely which crew, province, or single site is driving any cost or schedule deviation — not just whether the programme overall is on track.
